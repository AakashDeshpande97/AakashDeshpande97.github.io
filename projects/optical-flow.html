Classical and deep optical flow on Fourier ptychographic microscopy

TypeResearch project
WhenAug 2024 – Jul 2025
ContextUniversity of Siegen
View code on GitHub

Overview

How well do motion-estimation methods work on microscope images? Using leech images from a Fourier ptychographic microscope, I compared classical optical flow algorithms with modern deep networks, then checked each result by warping the images and measuring how well they line up. Supervised by Dr. John Meshreki.

2 + 20classical methods, and 20+ pretrained deep models available through PTLFlow
~80 imagesper crop grid, each aligned to the centre tile
Lower MAEafter warping, for every method

How it works

  1. Prepare dataBrightfield images extracted from EXR and standardised to grayscale PNG.
  2. Classical flowHorn–Schunck and TV-L1, multi-scale, with tuned parameters.
  3. Deep flowRAFT, CRAFT, PWC-Net, FlowNet2, LiteFlowNet and others via PTLFlow.
  4. VisualiseX and Y components, direction-and-magnitude colour maps, heatmaps.
  5. EvaluateWarp each image to the reference and compare MAE before and after.

What I did

  • Built a pipeline that processes whole grids of microscope images in batches, with the centre tile as the reference frame.
  • Used the PTLFlow framework (PyTorch Lightning) for inference with many pretrained models through one API.
  • Wrote post-processing to warp images with the predicted flow fields, and custom visualisations laid out to match the microscope's grid.

What I found

  • Horn–Schunck and TV-L1 were robust, interpretable baselines on this data.
  • Deep models gave promising results but sometimes produced motion that isn't there, because they were trained on natural images. Retraining on microscopy data would be the next step.
  • MAE dropped after warping for all methods, confirming the flow fields align the images.
